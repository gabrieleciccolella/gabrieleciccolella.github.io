Thesis Overview

This project was carried out as part of my Bachelor’s thesis in Industrial Engineering. The research focused on developing and evaluating various Machine Learning algorithms for the automatic recognition of rehabilitation exercises.

A set of physiotherapeutic exercises was selected and performed by multiple patients under controlled conditions. The resulting motion data were processed to build a comprehensive dataset used for model training and validation.

The goal was to assess each algorithm’s ability to accurately classify different exercises, ensuring robustness and generalization across individuals.

Methodology

  • Dataset: Constructed from kinematic and motion sensor data collected during rehabilitation sessions.
  • Features: Spatial–temporal descriptors representing limb trajectories and acceleration patterns.
  • Preprocessing: Missing-value handling, normalization, and feature scaling for consistency.
  • Models evaluated: LDA, Decision Tree, Logistic Regression, SVM, KNN, Random Forest, Gaussian NB, AdaBoost.
  • Metric: Accuracy (%), computed with consistent cross-validation splits and preprocessing.

Conclusions

The comparison of models revealed that ensemble and linear approaches yield the best generalization performance. Random Forest proved to be the most accurate and stable across diverse patient data.

This work highlights the potential of intelligent algorithms to support physiotherapists in monitoring rehabilitation progress and improving exercise quality assessment.
